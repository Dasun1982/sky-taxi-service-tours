Wildlife in Sri Lanka

An island with an unusual concentration of wildlife

Sri Lanka packs rainforest, dry-zone scrub, wetlands, and coastline into a small area — which is part of why its national parks hold such a wide range of animals within short private-driver reach of most major routes. Every photograph on this page is a genuine sighting, not a stock photo or an AI image.

Wildlife highlights

A closer look at what shares the island — from apex predators to the birdlife along every wetland edge.

Sri Lankan leopard

One of the highest leopard sighting densities of any safari destination on earth.

Asian elephant

Herds gathering at waterholes, especially visible in the drier months.

Sloth bear

Shy, shaggy-coated foragers, harder to spot than leopards or elephants.

Mugger crocodile

Found resting along rivers, lakes, and mangrove-lined waterways.

Golden jackal

Common across open grassland and wetland edges, often seen at dusk.

Painted stork

One of many wading birds working the shallows of Sri Lanka's wetlands.

Indian star tortoise

Its radiating shell pattern makes it one of the island's most distinctive reptiles.

Toque macaque

A primate found only in Sri Lanka, common in forest and temple grounds alike.

Ruddy mongoose

A quick, low-slung hunter often seen crossing forest tracks.

Oriental garden lizard

Common and colourful, often seen basking on branches near water.

Changeable hawk-eagle

A powerful raptor of Sri Lanka's forests and open country.

Common kingfisher

A flash of blue and orange along quiet streams and lagoons.

Green bee-eater

Slender, acrobatic, and common near open scrub and farmland.

Cormorants

Often seen nesting and drying their wings together in waterside trees.

Purple-rumped sunbird

A tiny, iridescent nectar-feeder found in gardens and forest edges.

Indian flap-shell turtle

A freshwater turtle regularly seen basking on rocks and logs.
